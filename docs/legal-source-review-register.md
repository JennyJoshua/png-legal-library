## Source review acceptance criteria
A source may be marked verified only after recording:
1. Institutional identity and official domain evidence.
2. Statutory or administrative role.
3. Precise document categories published.
4. Whether it is primary, an official copy, or a secondary repository.
5. Access date and working URL.
6. Terms of use, copyright and redistribution conditions.
7. Known coverage gaps and update frequency.
8. Reviewer identity and review date.

Source verification does not verify every document hosted by that source.

## Initial evidence notes
- Parliament's bills and legislation page describes legislative tracking, but its current public page displayed no results during the initial review. An empty result list does not prove no legislation exists.
- PMNEC's OLC page describes legislative advice, vetting, amendments, editing and printing of laws, including provincial and local-level legislation.
- CLRC describes its law-reform role. Keep reports, recommendations, directives and enacted laws in separate categories.
- The National Gazette is the official journal identified by the Constitution. Third-party scans are discovery copies until matched to the official issue and page.
- PacLII is valuable for legal research and discovery; it should not be the sole basis for current-law determinations where primary evidence is available.

## Maintenance
Review source availability at least quarterly and whenever an instrument is added or amended. Record failures and redirects; never silently replace an official source URL with an unreviewed mirror.
