# PNG National Legislation Inventory — Gazette and Amendment Verification Update

**Review date:** 3 November 2026  
**Branch:** `phase10/legal-corpus-foundation`  
**Scope:** Organic Law on Provincial Governments and Local-level Governments; District Development Authority Act and amendments.

## Executive result

This update incorporates a newly located 2026 National Court ruling that materially affects the NCD commencement analysis.

1. **Principal Organic Law:** PacLII's PNG laws index identifies the principal instrument as the Organic Law on Provincial Governments and Local-level Governments, **No. 16 of 1995**.
2. **No. 29 of 1998:** The same index separately identifies this as the **Organic Law Amendment No. 7 Law 1998**. The Ombudsman Commission's hosted page labels its displayed principal text “No. 29 of 1998”; this appears to be a numbering/metadata conflation. The page's blank certification field means it should not be treated as the definitive principal-law citation.
3. **NCD commencement:** Gazette G712/2021 originally fixed 21 October 2021 as commencement for NCD. Gazette G215/2023 later purported to correct that notice and defer commencement until the later of publication of the corrigendum or repeal of the NCDC Act 2001. A National Court ruling dated 24 February 2026 is reported to have held the corrigendum void ab initio. This strongly supports the original G712 notice remaining effective, but the official judgment and appeal/finality status must be checked before final legal approval.
4. **DDA Amendment 2021 No.14:** Commencement recorded as 1 January 2022 under National Gazette G80/2022.
5. **DDA Amendment 2015 No.37 and 2016 No.11:** PacLII's 14 April 2022 index marks both N.I.F. (not in force) at that date. No later commencement notice was established in this review. Do not state their status in 2026 as settled.
6. **DDA law reform:** CLRC announced on 13 August 2026 that it had launched a nationwide review of DDA laws under TOR No.26. This is a law-reform process, not an enacted amendment.

## 1. Organic Law: correct principal identity and amendment register

### NCD commencement: G712/2021, G215/2023 and the 2026 National Court ruling

- **G712/2021:** National Gazette No. G712, published 21 October 2021, contains the original Notice of Commencement for the Organic Law on Provincial Governments and Local-level Governments 1995 (No.16 of 1995), fixing the date of Gazette publication as commencement insofar as the Organic Law relates to NCD.
- **G215/2023:** National Gazette No. G215, published 28 March 2023, contains a corrigendum to G712. It purported to change the NCD commencement date to the date of publication of the corrigendum or the date of repeal of the National Capital District Commission Act 2001, whichever falls later.
- **Hanenamo Development Limited v National Capital District Commission and Independent State of PNG, N11736 (24 February 2026):** A legal database's report of this National Court interlocutory ruling says the Court held that the fixing of the date was delegated law-making under the Organic Law, and that the G215 corrigendum was void ab initio because it was not authorised by the Organic Law or sections 89–90 of the Interpretation Act. The ruling reportedly addresses whether the corrigendum could lawfully alter G712.

**Assessment:** The 2026 ruling materially undermines reliance on the G215 corrigendum and supports the original G712 commencement date of 21 October 2021 for NCD. However, the report is from a secondary database. Before treating this as final legal status, obtain the signed official judgment, confirm the exact orders and reasoning, and check whether an appeal or later decision changed the position. Keep the Organic Law record `status_unknown` until that legal check is completed.

### 2021 amendment

National Gazette G80, published 4 February 2022, contains a notice fixing 1 January 2022 as commencement for the 2021 amendment. The PacLII index corroborates this.

### 2015 amendment — identifier discrepancy

PacLII identifies the DDA Amendment Act 2015 as **No. 37 of 2015** and marks it N.I.F. as at 14 April 2022. FAOLEX separately lists a DDA Amendment Act 2015 as **No. 31 of 2015**. This discrepancy has not been resolved. The original parliamentary and Gazette copies must be compared before the repository assigns a definitive identifier or commencement status.

### 2016 amendment

PacLII identifies No. 11 of 2016 and records N.I.F. as at 14 April 2022. No later commencement notice was established in this pass. Its current status remains unknown.

### Current law reform

On 13 August 2026, the Constitutional and Law Reform Commission announced launch of the Issues Paper for Terms of Reference No.26 — Review of the Laws Relating to District Development Authorities. CLRC says the TOR was endorsed and gazetted by the Minister early in 2026 and that the review is intended to assess the DDA legislative framework and recommend reforms.

This is significant evidence that the framework is under active review. It does not itself amend the law or prove any proposed change has been enacted. The gazetted TOR, Issues Paper, consultation record and final report should be tracked separately.

## 5. Follow-up verification check — 3 November 2026

A further search of publicly indexed PNG Judiciary and PacLII materials did not locate an official signed copy of National Court ruling N11736 or a separate publicly indexed appeal/later-order record. The PNG Judiciary's National Court information page explains that the Registry maintains the central case database and holds the files and documents for proceedings; the Supreme Court Registry handles Supreme Court appeals. This search result is **not proof that no appeal or subsequent order exists**. The vLex report identifies N11736 as an interlocutory ruling on stated questions of law before trial, not final disposal of the underlying originating summons. The correct next verification step is to request a certified copy of the 24 February 2026 ruling and current docket/order history for OS No.298 of 2021 (CC1) from the National Court Registry at Waigani, and separately ask the Supreme Court Registry whether any appeal, review or related proceeding has been filed or decided. Until those records are obtained, retain `status_unknown` for the Organic Law and describe the G215 invalidity conclusion as a reported interlocutory ruling, not a final determination of NCDC's taxing powers.
